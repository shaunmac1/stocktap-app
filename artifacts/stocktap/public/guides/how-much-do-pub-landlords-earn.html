How much do pub landlords earn? What a tenant, a manager and a free house owner actually take home in 2026

Shaun McManus 13 November 2026

How much a pub landlord earns depends on which kind of landlord they are. A tied tenant running a £5,000-a-week wet-led pub on the BBPA's own cost model keeps about £371 a week before tax at a 50% rent, £19,292 a year, which is £7.42 an hour at 50 hours. A salaried pub manager is typically advertised at £35,000 to £40,000. A free house owner with no rent might keep about £300 a week on the same takings, before paying for the building.

"Landlord" covers three different jobs with three different pay packets, and most of the pages answering this mash them into one salary figure. Below is each route worked out from published numbers, what it comes to per hour, and the one sum I'd do before signing anything.

None of these is "the" answer. A busy food pub on a good lease can make its tenant far more than any salary on the list. A quiet local can pay its tenant nothing. But they're the honest starting points, and they come from the people who set rents and the people paying wages, not from a careers database.

Salaried managers: the easiest number to find

I read 19 live UK pub management job ads on 26 October 2026 for my pub manager salary guide. The common general manager salary was £35,000 to £40,000. Live-in roles started lower, at £28,725 per person with a rent-free flat. Premium pubs with rooms advertised packages up to £50,000 or £60,000 including profit share. The National Careers Service gives a publican £27,000 starting and £50,000 experienced, with typical hours of 45 to 50 a week.

The catch is the hours. Divide £35,000 by 52 weeks and 60 hours and it's £11.22 an hour, under the £12.71 National Living Wage. Salaried managers aren't exempt from the minimum wage. That guide has the table showing where each salary drops below it.

Tied tenants: the number nobody puts in the brochure

A tenant isn't paid. They keep what's left. The BBPA's guide to costs for tied tenants and lessees models a community wet-led pub taking about £5,000 a week. After the cost of the drink and every running cost except the tenant's own time, it leaves a divisible balance of £742 a week. The rent is a share of that. At 50%, the pubco takes £371 and the tenant keeps £371.

The BBPA guide says plainly that its models leave out managers' salaries, because in a tenancy the tenant is the manager. So that £371 is your wage, the return on the money you put in, and your cushion for a bad month, all at once. At 50 hours a week it's £7.42 an hour. At 60 it's £6.18. If it has to keep two of you, halve it. How that divisible balance is built and where it tends to go wrong for the tenant is all in how pub rent is calculated.

The flat upstairs is part of the deal, and it's worth something. But it's in the rent you're paying, not on top of what you earn.

Free house owners: no rent, but not free

money.co.uk's analysis of BBPA cost data, reported in March 2026, put pub profit at about 3p in every pound after rent, and about 6p before it. A free house owner who owns the building outright keeps the 6p: about £300 a week on £5,000 of takings, before tax. If there's a mortgage or a loan on the building, that comes out of the £300. That's why plenty of free house owners are on less than a tenant, at least until the debt is paid. The whole pound, line by line, is in how much profit does a pub make, and what "free house" means in practice is in what is a free house.

What moves a landlord's pay the most

When your income is what's left over, small percentages turn into big chunks of it. On the BBPA's £5,000 model:

What each change does to the tenant's £371 a week (my arithmetic on the BBPA model, rent held at £371)
ChangePer weekTenant keepsShare of tenant's pay
Drinks GP two points lower than the model (waste, line cleaning, unrung pints)-£97£27426%

Look at the first row. Two points of GP is a quarter of the tenant's pay. Vianet's data found about 2% of drinks poured never reach the till in an average pub, and losses like that, along with line cleaning and waste, are exactly what eats GP points. The rent split, the thing everyone argues about, matters less than the stock. If I could tell a new tenant one thing, it's that a monthly stocktake is how you find out what you're actually earning, rather than what the brochure said you would.

The sum to do before you sign

Take the pubco's own shadow profit and loss account for the pub, the one they built the rent from. Find the divisible balance, take off the rent they're asking, and divide what's left by the hours you'll really work. Not the hours you hope to work. Then compare it with what you'd pay someone to cover your shift.

If you'd earn more an hour as the relief manager of your own pub than as its tenant, the numbers behind the rent need a hard look. The Pubs Code gives you rights to the information behind a rent proposal if your pubco has 500 or more tied pubs, and the steps are in the Pubs Code before you sign. The broader route in, from training to the first stocktake, is in how to become a pub landlord.

Nineteen years behind the pumps tells me the people who do well out of this aren't the ones with the best rent. They're the ones who know their numbers every month and fix the small leaks before they're big ones.

Common questions

How much do pub landlords earn in the UK?

It depends on the deal. A tied tenant running a £5,000-a-week wet-led pub on the BBPA's own cost model keeps about £371 a week before tax at a 50% rent, £19,292 a year. A salaried pub manager is typically advertised at £35,000 to £40,000. The government careers site gives £27,000 starting and £50,000 experienced.

How much does a pub tenant make per hour?

On the BBPA's community wet-led model at a 50% rent, the tenant's £371 a week is £7.42 an hour at 50 hours and £6.18 at 60. The National Living Wage is £12.71, rising to £13.21 in April 2027.

Do pub landlords earn more than pub managers?

Not always. A manager on £35,000 working 50 hours earns about £13.46 an hour with no money at risk. A tenant on the BBPA model earns about £7.42 an hour, but keeps any improvement in trade and can build a business to sell if they hold an assignable lease.

How much profit does a free house make?

Analysis of BBPA cost data by money.co.uk put pub profit at about 6p in the pound before rent and 3p after. On a £5,000 week a free house owner with no rent might keep about £300 a week before tax, out of which any mortgage or loan has to be paid.

What is the biggest thing that changes a landlord's earnings?

Gross profit. On the BBPA £5,000 model, two points of drinks GP is £97 a week, about a quarter of the tenant's whole income. Stock loss, waste and pricing move a tenant's pay more than almost anything else they control.
