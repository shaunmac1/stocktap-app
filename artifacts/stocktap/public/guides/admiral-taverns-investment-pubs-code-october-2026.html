Admiral Taverns' £38.4m, 14 October 2026: before you take pubco investment money, read the Pubs Code's investment exception

Shaun McManus 14 October 2026

Admiral Taverns told the Morning Advertiser this morning that it will spend £38.4m on its pubs this financial year, up from £34.1m last year, and more than £100m over three years. Its boss says success would mean another 200 pubs getting meaningful investment over the coming year. If you're an Admiral tenant, or tied to any of the six big pubcos, and the BDM turns up with drawings and a budget, the money is the easy bit. The bit to read first is regulation 56 of the Pubs Code, the investment exception. A deal that meets it can switch off your right to ask for a free-of-tie rent at your next renewal or rent assessment for up to seven years.

What Admiral announced

From the Morning Advertiser's interview with chief executive Chris Jowsey, 14 October 2026:

  • £38.4m of capital spending this financial year, against £34.1m last year. That's a rise of about 12.6%, my sum.
  • Around £8.5m of it goes on maintenance. Last year roughly three quarters of the spend went on growth projects, and the split is broadly the same this year.
  • In Proper Pubs, the operator-managed side, an extra £600,000 goes on operator incentives for driving sales, improving yields and reducing wastage.
  • Jowsey says almost 40% of pub revenue goes in tax one way or another, and that's why Admiral isn't spending more. That lines up almost exactly with the 39.8% in Wetherspoon's accounts this month.

Fair play to them. A pubco spending more while trading's this hard is good news, and a pub done properly can trade like a different business. Nothing below is a dig at Admiral. It applies to whichever regulated pubco you're tied to.

The rule nobody mentions in the pitch

Under the Pubs Code you can ask for a market rent only offer, MRO, which is your route to going free of tie, at four points. A significant price increase, a trigger event, the renewal of your agreement, and a rent assessment. My MRO step-by-step guide covers all four.

Regulation 23 says the last two, renewal and rent assessment, are off the table while the investment exception applies. Those are the two most tenants actually plan around. The price-increase and trigger-event routes stay open.

The exception only applies when the deal ticks every box in regulation 56:

If the pubco didn't give you that forecast information, the agreement has no effect for the exception and you can refer it to the Pubs Code Adjudicator. Same if the works run late without a proper new date, or if you and the pubco disagree about whether it's an investment agreement at all.

What I'd check before signing

Do the twice-the-rent sum yourself. Last full year's rent, times two. If the spend is under that, it isn't an investment exception deal, whatever anyone calls it.

Split the repairs out. Admiral says it often rolls maintenance into upgrades, which is sensible. But if the pub needs work the pubco has to do anyway, ask for the list split in two, in writing. Only the second half is investment.

Ask why that length. Seven years is the maximum, not the default. Ask how they got to the number.

Test their forecast against your own numbers. The two-year profit and loss is their projection. You need your own sales, your own GP% and your own stock loss to check it. If it assumes a yield your cellar has never managed, you want to know before the rent review lands. A run of honest stocktakes is your evidence.

Know the rent review date. The Code makes them put it in the agreement. Put it in your diary with what the rent assessment rules let you ask for. My rent assessment guide has the timings.

None of this is legal advice. The Code wants you to take independent professional advice before signing, and on a deal this size you should.

My take

Investment is how a lot of tired pubs get a second life, and I'd rather see a pubco spending than sitting on its hands. But an investment exception deal is a trade. Their money, for a stretch where two of your four MRO routes are closed. Take it with your eyes open, your own numbers in your hand and a review date you can live with.

And keep counting through the works. A refurb is when stock walks out in boxes, lines get left half full and nobody can say what was there before the builders came. My refurbishment stocktake checklist covers that bit.

Common questions

What is the Pubs Code investment exception?

It's regulation 56 of the Pubs Code. Where a regulated pubco makes a qualifying investment in a tied pub, worth at least twice the annual rent, under a written investment agreement, the tenant can't ask for a market rent only offer at a renewal or a rent assessment until the investment period ends. The period must be reasonable for the size of the investment and can't be longer than seven years.

Can I still go free of tie after taking pubco investment?

Partly. While the investment exception applies, the renewal and rent assessment routes to an MRO offer are closed. A significant price increase or a trigger event can still let you serve an MRO notice, within 21 days starting the day after the event.

How much is Admiral Taverns investing in its pubs?

£38.4m in its 2027 financial year, up from £34.1m the year before, and more than £100m over three years, according to chief executive Chris Jowsey in the Morning Advertiser on 14 October 2026. Around £8.5m of this year's budget is for maintenance.

What must the pubco give me before an investment agreement?

Information showing how the investment would be expected to change the pub and increase its trade and profit, such as a reasonable two-year forecast profit and loss, based on a reasonably efficient tenant. If it doesn't, the agreement has no effect for the investment exception, and you can refer that to the Pubs Code Adjudicator.
