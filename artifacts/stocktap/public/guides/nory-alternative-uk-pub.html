Nory Alternative for UK Pubs: What It Actually Costs to Find Out

Shaun McManus Licensee, Washington, Tyne and Wear 31 July 2026

If you’ve tried to get a straight answer on what Nory costs, you’ll know the feeling: you land on nory.ai, get invited to “book a chat,” and that’s it. No tiers, no per-site rate, no ballpark. For a pub licensee trying to compare three or four tools on a Tuesday night after close, that’s not a minor inconvenience — it’s a wall.

I run a single-site pub in Washington, Tyne and Wear, and I’ve been through this exercise myself: trying to work out what stock and ordering software actually costs before I’d sunk an afternoon into a sales call. So here’s what’s actually knowable about Nory’s pricing right now, what a UK wet-led or food-led pub should expect to pay for something in its category, and where the gaps are.

What Nory is

Nory is an AI-driven back-of-house platform aimed mainly at multi-site restaurant and hospitality groups — rota/labour forecasting, stock and inventory, and financial reporting rolled into one system. It’s a genuinely capable tool for a group operator with several sites and a head office finance function. The question is whether it’s built, or priced, for a single pub.

The pricing wall

As of 31 July 2026:

  • nory.ai has no public pricing page. The commercial page routes straight to a “book a chat” booking form.
  • Capterra’s Nory listing (capterra.com/p/235524/Nory/) confirms: starting price “Contact vendor,” no free trial, no free version.
  • The only published number anywhere is on a competitor’s own comparison page — MarketMan’s site (marketman.com/compare/marketman-vs-nory) lists Nory at £285/month against MarketMan’s own £276/month. That’s a competitor’s marketing page, not a Nory-published rate, and it doesn’t say what tier, site count, or contract length that £285 assumes. Treat it as directionally interesting, not something you’d budget against.

For a licensee, “contact vendor” pricing almost always signals one of two things: enterprise-scale pricing that doesn’t suit a single site, or a sales process built around annual contracts negotiated site-by-site. Neither is wrong, but neither tells you what Tuesday’s stocktake software is going to cost you in March.

What you actually need this software to do

Strip away the AI-labour-forecasting layer aimed at multi-site restaurant groups, and what a single pub or small group genuinely needs from stock software is:

  • Fast, accurate stocktaking against actual sales
  • Variance reporting you can act on before the next delivery, not after
  • A price that doesn’t require a sales call to discover
  • No 36-month tie-in for a tool you haven’t used yet

How to actually compare Nory against alternatives

Since Nory won’t give you a number without a call, here’s how to run the comparison properly before you pick up the phone:

  1. Ask for the number in writing before the demo, not during it. If a vendor won’t put a per-site monthly rate in an email, that’s information in itself.
  2. Ask what happens at renewal. Multi-site AI platforms often quote an intro rate that steps up after year one — get the year-two number before you sign anything.
  3. Ask about minimum term. Some hospitality-tech platforms in this space use 12- to 36-month minimum terms (see our Access Procure Wizard piece for what that can cost in practice). Get this in writing, not verbally.
  4. Compare against tools built for single-site pubs, not just multi-site groups. A platform designed for a 40-site restaurant group’s rota-and-labour problem is solving a different problem than a single pub’s stock-variance problem.

The honest verdict

Nory looks like solid kit for a multi-site restaurant group with a finance team behind it. For a single pub trying to get stock and variance right without a lengthy sales process, the total absence of public pricing is itself the main strike against it — you can’t compare what you can’t see. If price transparency and a quick start matter more to you than enterprise-grade labour forecasting, it’s worth trying software that shows you the number on the website.

Common questions

Does Nory publish any pricing at all?

No. As of 31 July 2026, nory.ai routes all pricing enquiries to a “book a chat” call, and its Capterra listing confirms “Contact vendor” with no free trial and no free version.

Is the £285/month figure real?

It appears only on a competitor’s (MarketMan’s) own comparison page, not on anything Nory has published. It may reflect a specific tier or site count that isn’t disclosed, so treat it as an unconfirmed data point, not a quote you can budget against.

Does Nory offer a free trial?

No — Capterra explicitly lists “Free trial not available” for Nory as of this check.

Is Nory built for single pubs or multi-site groups?

Its feature set (AI labour forecasting across sites, group-level financial reporting) is aimed primarily at multi-site restaurant and hospitality operators, not single-site pubs.
