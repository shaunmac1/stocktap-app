Running the pub

Pub manager salary UK 2026: what 19 real job ads pay, and the hours where it drops below minimum wage

Shaun McManus 26 October 2026

A full-time pub general manager in the UK is being offered about £35,000 to £40,000 in most job ads right now, with premium food-led pubs and pubs with rooms advertising up to £50,000 or £60,000 including profit share. Live-in roles start lower, around £28,700 a person with a rent-free flat. Assistant managers sit around £31,000 to £32,000 outside London. Those are the headline figures from 19 live UK job ads I read on 26 October 2026. The figure the ads don't give you is the hours, and that's where a salary can quietly drop below the minimum wage. At £35,000 that happens past 53 hours a week.

Every salary page I found for this question is either a guess from a database that mixes pub managers with publishing managers, or a careers profile with a range and no date. So I went and read the ads. Real employers and real numbers from this week. Then I did the sum nobody in the trade likes doing out loud: divide the salary by the hours you'll actually work.

What 19 job ads paid this week

I searched Indeed for pub manager, pub general manager, assistant pub manager and live-in roles across the UK on 26 October 2026 and read every ad in full. 13 of the 19 put a number on it. Three big-brand general manager ads listed a bonus scheme, discounts and private medical but no salary at all, which tells you something on its own.

A snapshot, not a survey. 19 ads on one day won't tell you the national average to the pound, and the ads that hide the salary are probably not hiding a big one. But it's what's actually on offer this week, which is the number you need if you're applying or hiring.

The sum the ads leave out

Since 1 April 2026 the National Living Wage is £12.71 an hour for anyone 21 and over. A salaried manager is not exempt from it. The test is your pay divided by the hours you actually work, not the hours on the contract, and HMRC has a whole section of its minimum wage manual on how salaried hours are counted, including what happens when you go over your contracted hours.

So here's each common salary turned into a weekly figure and the number of hours a week at which it stops covering £12.71. My arithmetic, a quick weekly check rather than HMRC's full calculation.

Look at the £35,000 line. That's the most common general manager salary in the ads. One of those ads said, to its credit, that great pubs shouldn't depend on managers working 60 to 70 hours a week. Fair enough, because at 60 hours a £35,000 salary works out at £11.22 an hour. At 55 hours it's £12.24. Both under the legal minimum for a 21-year-old glass collector.

And the government's own National Careers Service profile for a publican gives a starting salary of £27,000 and typical hours of 45 to 50 a week. Put those two together and you get £10.38 to £11.54 an hour. The figure is probably older than the April rise, but it's what a school leaver reading about the job will see.

None of this means every employer on that list is breaking the law. Contracts set basic hours, holidays reduce the weeks worked, and the full HMRC calculation runs over the year. It means the salary on its own tells you nothing. The salary and the hours together tell you everything.

Live-in jobs: what the flat is worth to the minimum wage

A free flat above the pub is worth a lot to you. It's worth very little to the minimum wage calculation. If the accommodation is free, your employer can count the accommodation offset towards your pay, and from April 2026 that's £11.10 a day, £77.70 a week. That's the cap whatever the flat would cost to rent. A three-bed flat in a town centre and a box room count the same.

That's why the £28,725 live-in salary covers 49.6 hours a week with the offset and 43.5 without. If the employer charges you rent above the offset rate instead, the excess comes off your pay for minimum wage purposes, which pushes the hourly figure down further.

Two more things in live-in ads worth reading slowly. "Per person" matters: £28,725 each for a couple is very different from £29,000 to £31,000 between you, and one of the joint ads didn't say which. Ask. And a fidelity bond paid before you start, £1,000 in two of this week's ads, is your money held by them, so ask in writing when and how it comes back.

When the "salary" isn't a salary

One ad showed £30,000 to £40,000 a year. Read to the end and the deal is a percentage of the pub's net turnover, paid to a limited company you set up, out of which you pay yourself and your staff, plus the council tax on the flat. That's a legitimate way of running pubs, but it isn't a job with a salary. It's a small business where the stock and bills are covered and your income depends on the till.

If that's what you're looking at, the useful question isn't "what's the salary". It's what the percentage would have paid the last operator after wages, on last year's actual takings. Get that in writing. Then compare it with what a tenant takes home, which on the industry's own model rent is about £7.42 an hour for the tenant's own labour. I worked that one through in how a pub rent is calculated.

If you're hiring a manager

Put the hours in the ad. Two of this week's best-paid ads said five days a week, and it reads like a benefit because in this trade it is one. It also protects you. A salary that's comfortably legal at 45 hours can stop being legal at 58, and the manager who's covering every short-staffed night is the one most likely to end up there. The 2026 wage rise sums and holiday pay rules are worth a look before you set the figure, and the employment law changes from this week and next year change the probation picture too.

Profit share is the other thing that keeps coming up. Four of the 13 ads with a figure mention it. It's a good idea when the manager can see the numbers it's paid on. If the share depends on GP, the manager needs a stocktake they trust, every period, or the bonus becomes an argument. Same problem as a kitchen GP bonus.

One idea worth trying

Whichever side of the bar you're on, keep an hours diary for the first four weeks. Start time, finish time, every day, including the twenty minutes cashing up and the Sunday morning delivery. Then divide four weeks' pay by the total. It takes a minute a day and it settles the question with a number instead of a feeling. For a manager it's the evidence if the hours creep. For an owner it's the warning before the hours creep into a minimum wage problem, and it usually shows exactly which shift needs another body on it.

Common questions

How much does a pub manager earn in the UK in 2026?

In 19 UK pub management job ads read on 26 October 2026, most general manager roles advertised £35,000 to £40,000, with premium food-led pubs and pubs with rooms offering up to £50,000 or £60,000 including profit share. Live-in roles started around £28,725 a person with a rent-free flat.

How much does a live-in pub manager earn?

This week's live-in ads offered £28,725 per person with a rent-free flat and bills paid, and £29,000 to £31,000 for a couple running a bar together. Always ask whether a joint salary is each or between you.

Is a salaried pub manager entitled to the minimum wage?

Yes. A salaried manager aged 21 or over must average at least the £12.71 National Living Wage for the hours actually worked. A £35,000 salary drops below that at about 53 hours a week.

Does free accommodation count towards the minimum wage?

Only up to the accommodation offset, which from April 2026 is £11.10 a day or £77.70 a week, whatever the flat would cost to rent. If rent is charged above that, the excess reduces pay for minimum wage purposes.

How much does an assistant pub manager earn?

Assistant manager ads read on 26 October 2026 offered £31,500 to £32,000 in Leicester and £45,000 to £47,000 at a London gastro pub group, usually with a share of tips or tronc.
