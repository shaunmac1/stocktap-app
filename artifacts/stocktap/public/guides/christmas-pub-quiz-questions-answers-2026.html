Running the pub

Christmas pub quiz questions and answers for 2026: 50 free questions, ready to read out

Shaun McManus 5 November 2026

Here are 50 Christmas pub quiz questions and answers for 2026, in five rounds of ten plus a tie-breaker, written to be read out loud in a pub and checked before they went up. They're free to use, no sign-up, no catch. Underneath them is how to run the night so it actually makes money: about £13 to put on if you host it yourself, six extra pints to cover it, and the one rule on jackpots that catches pubs out.

Search for one and you get the same 25 or 27 questions copied across a stack of local newspaper websites, half of them about a film nobody under fifty has seen. These are written for a pub crowd: one round of telly, one of Christmas songs, one of food and drink, one pub round, one of traditions. Easy enough that every team scores, hard enough that the tie-breaker matters. Read them straight off your phone or print the page.

Tie-breaker

In what year was the first commercial Christmas card sent, commissioned by Sir Henry Cole? Answer: 1843. Nearest wins.

Rounds 2 and 4 are spoken questions about songs and beer, so you're not playing any music. If you add a proper music round with clips, that's a different matter for licensing, covered below.

How to run it on the night

Five rounds of ten, read twice each, with a minute between questions, takes about 15 minutes a round including marking. So call it an hour and a half with a break after round three. That break is where your money is, so make it a proper one: ten minutes, last orders called for the round, not a pause while you find your glasses.

Swap answer sheets between teams to mark. Read the answers out, and keep this page open so nobody can argue with you. If someone does, the answer is "the quizmaster's decision is final", said with a smile.

Teams of up to six. Any bigger and one table wins every week and the rest stop coming, which is the quickest way to kill a quiz in January.

What it costs, and what it needs to make

I worked this through properly in does a pub quiz make money. The short version: these questions are free, so a self-hosted Christmas quiz is basically the prize. A £25 bar tab costs you about £9.40 in drink once you take the VAT and your margin off. At about £2.23 of gross profit on a standard lager, that's four or five extra pints to cover it. Add a printed answer sheet and a few quid of mince pies and it's still under the £13, six pints, the guide works to.

Two things to get right. An entry fee is standard-rated for VAT, so £1 at the door is about 83p to you. And ring the bar tab prize through the till when it's used, or it'll show up in your next stocktake as missing stock.

The jackpot rule

A quiz is legal to charge for because it's a competition decided by knowledge. A rollover jackpot won by drawing a card or picking an envelope isn't knowledge, it's chance, and that turns it into gambling with its own rules. If you want a Christmas jackpot, roll over the tie-breaker instead: a nearest-the-number question is skill, and you can let it build. The limits for raffles, bingo and the rest are in the games night rules guide, and if you're thinking about a festive raffle on the same night, read it before you sell a ticket.

If you add a music round

Playing clips is playing music in public. PPL PRS says you'll usually need TheMusicLicence for music segments in a quiz, and PRS prices a music quiz as its own session. The background radio licence most pubs already hold may not cover it. My music licence guide has the figures. The safe option is the one above: questions about songs, no songs played.

One idea for this Christmas

Sell the quiz to local workplaces as their Christmas do. Plenty of small firms, the garage, the dentist, the estate agents, want something for the team that isn't a £40-a-head sit-down meal. A quiz on a Tuesday in early December, a table booked in the firm's name, a bit of food, and a prize for the winning office. Take the organiser's name and email when they book. That list is your Christmas party bookings for next year, from people who've already had a good night in your pub. It costs nothing to ask, and early December is when your midweek nights need it.

Common questions

Where can I get free Christmas pub quiz questions?

This page has 50 free Christmas pub quiz questions and answers in five rounds of ten, plus a tie-breaker, written to be read out in a pub. No sign-up needed.

How many questions should a Christmas pub quiz have?

Forty to fifty is about right for a pub night. Five rounds of ten, read twice with a minute between questions and time to mark, runs about an hour and a half with a break.

Can a pub charge an entry fee for a Christmas quiz?

Yes. A quiz decided by knowledge is a prize competition, not gambling, so you can charge. The entry fee is standard-rated for VAT, so £1 is about 83p to the pub.

Can a pub quiz have a rollover jackpot?

Only if it's won by skill, such as a nearest-the-number tie-breaker. A jackpot won by drawing a card or picking an envelope is chance, which brings in gambling rules.

Do I need a music licence for a Christmas music round?

If you play music clips, PPL PRS says you'll usually need TheMusicLicence for that segment. Spoken questions about songs, with nothing played, don't involve playing music.
