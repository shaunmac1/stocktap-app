Running the pub

How to run a bingo night in a pub: the 2026 rules, the three traps, and all 90 traditional calls

Shaun McManus 29 October 2026

A pub can run bingo without any licence as long as it charges no entry fee, takes no cut, gives every penny staked back as prizes, caps stakes at £5 a person a game, keeps under-18s out, and stays under £2,000 a week in stakes or prizes. The house makes nothing on the game itself. The money is the bar. That's the whole legal shape, and the Gambling Commission updated its pubs page on 6 October 2026 with the same rules.

What catches pubs out isn't the headline. It's three smaller things almost nobody mentions: the "bingo and a curry for a tenner" ticket, taking card payments for books, and any game that links your pub to another one. All three are below, then the sums, then every traditional call from Kelly's eye to top of the shop, free to print.

The rules on one page

Bingo in a pub is "exempt gaming" under section 279 of the Gambling Act 2005. The section sets five conditions, the 2007 regulations set the stake limit, and the Commission's code of practice adds how it has to be run. Put together:

Games can only run while you can sell alcohol under your premises licence, according to Poppleston Allen's guidance for pubs. So bingo before opening time is out.

The three traps

The "bingo and a curry" ticket. It's the most common idea and it's a participation fee. A single price that gets you food, a drink and a bingo card is charging to play, and Poppleston Allen's guidance gives that exact example as not allowed. The fix is easy. Sell the curry. Keep the bingo free or stake-only, paid separately, all back as prizes. The Commission's page goes further: if you charge admission for a night with other entertainment on, people must be able to play bingo without paying that entry fee.

Card payments for books. The Commission's code of practice says payments for the gaming should be paid in cash before the game starts. Most pubs now take everything on card, so it's an easy one to break without thinking. Run bingo from a separate cash float, not through the till, and you also keep the bingo money out of your takings, which makes the cash-up cleaner.

Linked games. Section 279 says a game on one set of premises can't be linked to a game on another. That rules out a shared jackpot across a few local pubs, and it's worth remembering before you sign up to any app or online game that pools players from more than one venue. Inside your own four walls, phones, paper or a board on the telly are all fine. Across two pubs, it isn't exempt gaming any more.

Can you run free bingo with a prize from the pub?

Yes. Poppleston Allen's guidance for pubs says there's no limit on the value of prizes for each game and you can add prizes in excess of the stakes received. So free-to-play bingo with a bar tab for a full house is fine, and so is £1 a card with a bottle of something added to the pot. The £2,000 a week limit still counts prizes, so a big weekly jackpot can push you over it on its own.

A prize also costs you less than its face value. A £20 bar tab is 3.7 pints at £5.34. Each one costs you £2.00 in stock once you take off VAT and the £2.45 gross profit, so the tab costs about £7.50. In England and Wales you can give alcohol as a prize. In Scotland you can't, because the licensing conditions there ban promotions with alcohol as a prize, which I covered in the match day guide. Use a food voucher or a meal for two north of the border.

Does a bingo night pay?

Here's a rough sum, mine, not a real pub. A Thursday free bingo night with an extra member of staff for three hours and a £20 bar tab as the prize:

Extra staff, 3 hours at £12.71£38.13
£20 bar tab prize, at stock cost£7.50
Total£45.63
Extra pints to cover it, at £2.45 gross profit each19

19 extra pints is about ten people who wouldn't otherwise have come in on a Thursday, having two each. If you call it yourself and don't need the extra pair of hands, you're covering the prize with three pints. It's the same shape as my quiz night sums, and the same rule decides it: does it bring in people who weren't coming, or just give your regulars something to do with the drink they'd have bought anyway? Only the first pays.

If you'd rather run stake bingo, the sum's the same plus a pot that goes straight back out. 30 players at £1 a card, three games, is £90 staked and £90 paid out. That's £90 a week towards your £2,000, nowhere near it.

How to run it on the night

  • Pick 90-ball. It's the British pub game, the one people know the calls for. 75-ball is the American version.
  • Print the rules. One sheet on the board: free or £1 a card, five cards max per game, adults only, all stakes paid back as prizes, what wins a line and a full house. That covers the code's display requirement.
  • Keep a cash float and a two-line log. Each night write down total staked and total paid out, and keep a running seven-day total. It takes ten seconds, and it's your evidence if anyone ever asks how you know you're under £2,000.
  • Put the board on the telly. A number board on the screen lets the back of the room keep up, cuts the "what was that?" shouts, and helps the people who can't hear the caller over the bar. If you've got the screens anyway, use them.
  • Call slowly, and say the number twice. "On its own, number nine, doctor's orders, nine." The calls are half the fun and the reason people come.
  • Check the claim against the board out loud. Every time, even when it's obviously right. It's what stops the arguments.

Music bingo, where you play the songs instead of calling numbers, is a game of chance with prizes too, so I'd run it to the same rules. It also means playing recorded music, so check your music licence covers it. My music licence guide has the 2026 TheMusicLicence costs.

One idea worth trying

Build the bar break into the game. Most callers run the line and the full house back to back, and the bar sits idle while everyone's heads are down. Pay out the line, then call a ten-minute break before the full house: "get your drinks in, eyes down again at quarter past." Three games a night gives you three breaks, and that's where most of the 19 pints come from. It costs nothing, and players like it because nobody wants to queue mid-game and miss a number.

My take

Bingo's the cheapest entertainment night a pub can run, and the law is friendlier than people fear, as long as you remember the house can't make a penny on the game. Keep it free or stake-only, take cash for it, keep it in your own building, and write the totals down. The profit's at the bar, and on a wet Thursday in January, 19 pints is a good night.

Common questions

Do you need a licence to run bingo in a pub?

No, as long as it's exempt gaming: no participation fee, no cut from stakes or prizes, stakes up to £5 a person a game, all stakes returned as prizes, not linked to another venue, no under-18s, and under £2,000 in stakes or prizes in any seven days.

Can a pub charge an entry fee for bingo?

No. Pubs can't charge a participation or admission fee for bingo. A single ticket that includes food and a bingo card counts as a participation fee. Sell the food separately and keep the bingo free or stake-only.

What is the £2,000 bingo limit for pubs?

If the bingo played in any seven days goes over £2,000 in stakes or prizes, it's high turnover bingo. You must tell the Gambling Commission, and if it happens again within a year you need a bingo operating licence.

Can a pub add its own prizes to bingo?

Yes. Licensing solicitors Poppleston Allen say there's no limit on prizes per game and you can add prizes over the stakes received. Prizes still count towards the £2,000 a week limit. In Scotland, alcohol can't be offered as a prize.

Can I take card payments for pub bingo?

The Gambling Commission's code of practice says payments for the gaming should be in cash before the game starts, with no credit. Run bingo from a separate cash float rather than through the till.

What are the bingo calls from 1 to 90?

The traditional calls run from Kelly's eye for 1 and one little duck for 2, through legs eleven, two little ducks for 22 and clickety click for 66, to two fat ladies for 88 and top of the shop for 90. The full list is in the table above.
