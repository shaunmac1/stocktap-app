Looking at WISK? What $249 a month buys, the $750 that comes first, and the alternatives from £0 to £129

Shaun McManus 6 September 2026

WISK might be the most capable bar inventory system on the market, and it's the only big rival that takes weighing bottles as seriously as I do. It's also priced in US dollars at $249 a month for a single venue, there's a mandatory onboarding fee of $750 before you start, and there's no free trial. For a UK pub that's roughly £2,700 in year one, against alternatives that run from a free spreadsheet to £129 a month. Here's what all that money buys, who it makes sense for, and who it doesn't.

Usual disclosure first. I'm a licensee in Washington, Tyne and Wear, StockTap is my product, and this page compares mine with theirs. So I'll stick to what WISK publishes, credit what's good, and be plain about the cases where I'd point you at them rather than me. There's no trade-written WISK alternatives page for UK operators anywhere, just WISK's own comparison pages and software directories, so here's one from someone who actually counts stock for a living.

What WISK actually is

Going off wisk.ai as of 6 September 2026: a food and beverage inventory platform out of North America, built for bars, restaurants and hotels. The Single Venue tier is $249 a month for beverage or food, or $399 for both, and it's capped at 1,000 products. Multi-outlet properties are priced in bands, $499 a month for 2 to 3 outlets up to $1,250 for 8 to 10 on one product path, roughly double for the food and drink bundle. Above that sits an Enterprise tier for 11-plus outlets on custom pricing. Annual billing takes 17% off and includes done-for-you recipe data entry, which they value at over $1,000.

Then the fee their pricing page is admirably blunt about: every plan carries an onboarding charge. $750 for a single venue, $1,250 if you take the bundle, rising to $7,500-plus at enterprise level. I'll give them credit for printing it rather than burying it in the sales call. It's still $750 before you've counted a bottle, and one line on their own feature pages still says "no setup required", so the website and the invoice aren't quite telling the same story. Their FAQ is clear on the other thing too: there is no free trial. You get a demo and a consultation instead.

The counting is the interesting bit. You scan a barcode, put the open bottle on a Bluetooth scale, and WISK works out the contents from a database of 200,000 bottles with empty and full weights already in it. No scale to hand, and there's a visual mode where you confirm roughly how full the bottle looks. It claims counts up to five times faster and accuracy up to 99.7%, several people can count at once, invoice scanning updates your cost prices, and it integrates with somewhere north of 50 till systems, their pages say 50-plus in one place and over 60 in another. There's a full recipe and menu costing side as well.

Credit where it's due

I'll say this straight: WISK is the only major vendor whose counting method I'd defend in an argument. A barcode tells an app that a bottle exists. A scale tells it what's actually in the bottle, and that difference is the whole reason variance lines argue with reality. I built my product around weighing for exactly this reason, so watching one of the biggest names in the category commit to it properly, with a bottle-weight database that saves you weighing empties yourself, is the opposite of annoying. It's the right answer.

The food side is real too. Recipes, sub-recipes, menu costing from live ingredient prices, the machinery a kitchen actually needs. I went through why kitchen and bar stock are two different jobs in the restaurant guide, and WISK is one of the few products that does both jobs properly instead of doing one and gesturing at the other. If you run a serious food operation across multiple outlets, that matters.

And the multi-outlet banding is honest pricing. Hotels, casino floors, groups with a central stockroom, that's plainly who the product is built for, and their own tier names say so. I covered the maths of running several sites in the multi-site guide, and for a 6-bar hotel WISK's $899 band is at least a price you can read without booking a call, which is more than most enterprise vendors manage.

The dollars problem

Now the part their pricing page can't help you with. Every figure on it is in US dollars, and you'll pay in dollars from a UK bank account. At the exchange rate as I write, $249 a month lands somewhere around £185, but the honest version is that your subscription cost now moves with the currency markets, and your card provider takes its slice of every payment. Year one for a single wet-led venue, $249 times twelve plus the $750 onboarding, is $3,738. Call it £2,700 and change, before any conversion fees.

The 1,000-product cap on Single Venue deserves a look too. My own library is under 400 products, so a normal pub fits with room to spare. A pub that does proper food, or a bar with a deep back catalogue of whiskies and wines, should count its lines before assuming. Their capability table also caps AI invoice scans per month by tier, so if your invoice volume is heavy, ask where your numbers land before you sign.

And there's no trial, which changes how you have to shop. The only test of stock software that means anything is two full counts with real trading in between, I set the method out in the free trial guide, and with WISK you can't run that test without paying $750 plus a month's subscription first. The demo will be polished. Demos always are. What a demo can't show you is your own variance.

The pints arithmetic

Price every subscription in gross profit, because gross profit is what pays for it. At the Morning Advertiser's 2026 average lager price of £4.89, a pint leaves about £2.24 of GP after VAT at a 55% margin. WISK at roughly £185 a month needs about 19 pints of GP a week before it has saved you anything, and the $399 bundle needs about 30. Jelly needs 13, Growyze Pro needs 8, StockTap Premium needs 4. Spread the $750 onboarding over the first year and WISK's real first-year hurdle is more like 24 pints a week. None of that settles it on its own, a system that catches losses you can't see pays for itself fast, and the ROI guide shows the payback method properly. But know your number before the demo, not after.

So who should actually buy WISK?

Straight answer. If you run a hotel with several bars, a casino floor, or a food-led group across outlets, you want recipes and purchasing and counting in one system, and a few hundred dollars a month per property is a rounding error against your stockholding, WISK is a credible buy and probably the strongest product in that bracket. Take the hotel bar guide with you and make the demo walk your worst outlet, not their best one.

If you're a single-site UK pub or bar, I struggle to make the sums work. You'd be paying hotel-group money, in a foreign currency, with a $750 entry fee and no trial, largely for a counting method you can get for £39 a month using the scale you already own. The weighing philosophy is right, it's the price of admission that's wrong for this end of the market. Run the two-count test on something you can start free, take the buyer's checklist into any demo you do book, and make every vendor, me included, show you an open bottle going from shelf to number.

Common questions

How much does WISK cost in the UK?

WISK publishes US dollar pricing only: $249 a month for a single venue on beverage or food, $399 for both, plus a mandatory onboarding fee of $750 to $1,250, as of 6 September 2026. Multi-outlet bands run $499 to $1,999 a month. UK buyers pay in dollars, so the pound cost moves with the exchange rate, roughly £185 a month for the entry tier as I write. Confirm current terms with the vendor.

Does WISK have a free trial?

No. WISK's own FAQ says it does not offer a free trial and provides demos and consultations instead. That means you can't run the two-stocktakes-with-a-month-between test before committing money, which is the only trial method that really proves stock software against your own variance.

How does WISK count stock?

By barcode scan plus a Bluetooth scale for open bottles, backed by a database of around 200,000 bottles with empty and full weights, with a visual fallback mode where you confirm how full a bottle looks. Weight-based counting is the accurate way to do open stock, and WISK is the only major platform besides StockTap built around it.

Is WISK priced in pounds for UK venues?

No. All published pricing is in US dollars, including for UK buyers, so your monthly cost varies with the exchange rate and any card conversion fees. There is no published UK price list as of September 2026.

What are the main WISK alternatives for a UK venue?

Jelly at £129 a location flat, Growyze at £79 to £139 a venue with a 30-day no-card trial, StockTap at £39 a month with weight-based counting for wet-led sites, and a well-built spreadsheet at £0. Quote-only enterprise tools like Craftable and Kitchen Cut sit above all of these.
