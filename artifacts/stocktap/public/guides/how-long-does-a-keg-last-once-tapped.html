Running the pub

How long does a keg last once tapped? Five days for keg, three for cask, and how to size your lines so you hit it

Shaun McManus 13 November 2026

Once a keg is tapped and on sale in a pub cellar, aim to empty it within about five days. That's Cask Marque's guidance, and Tennent's tells its pubs the same: after five days you'll notice changes in taste and aroma, and more fobbing or flat beer. Cask is shorter, about three days on sale.

That's for a proper pub set-up: keg on gas, cellar at 11 to 13°C, lines cleaned weekly. Party kegs and home taps are a different thing, follow the label. And the five days isn't a food safety rule. Nothing dramatic happens on day six. The beer just gets worse, fobs more, and you pour more of it down the drip tray, which is where the money goes.

Two different clocks, then. The best-before on the keg is for the sealed container sitting in your cellar. Once it's connected, the five days is the one that matters. How the dates work, and which drinks don't need one at all, is in out-of-date beer in a pub.

Why a keg goes downhill after five days

Keg beer is pushed out by gas, and the gas sitting on top of the beer keeps dissolving into it. Leave it long enough and the beer is carrying more gas than the system was set up for. It breaks out in the line and the glass, you get fob, and the first pint of every session is half froth. Cask Marque's point about turning the gas off between sessions is the same problem from the other end.

Cask has no gas on top. Once you vent it, air gets in as the beer comes out, and that's why its clock is shorter. A breather slows that down, which is the whole argument in cask breathers and cellar yield.

The pints-a-week column is the rate you'd need if the line sold at that pace all week. Real pubs don't, and that's the catch. A line doing 90 pints a week can still get through an 88 in five days if 60 of them go Friday to Sunday and you put the keg on Thursday. The same line, with the keg going on on a Monday, sits there until the weekend. When you put it on matters nearly as much as the size. What to do with a slow line

Every pub has one. The craft lager somebody asked for, the cider that sells in July and sits in November. Options, roughly in the order I'd try them:

  • Go down a size. If your supplier does the brand in 30 or 20 litres, a slow line on 20s is a fresher line. The price per pint is often a bit higher. It's usually cheaper than the beer you'd pour away.
  • Time the change. On a line that sells mostly at the weekend, aim to put a new one on Thursday or Friday, not Monday.
  • Swap it for packaged. A brand that sells 30 pints a week might be a better bottle or can than a tap.
  • Take the tap off. Vianet's data put the cost of an under-used tap at around £500 a year in quality and wastage. Fewer, busier lines is the subject of how many draught lines a pub needs, including the line count where it stops paying.

The day-dot check on stocktake day

This is the idea I'd steal, if I were you, because nobody seems to do it. When a keg goes on, stick a day dot or write the date on the collar. On stocktake day, walk the cellar and note how many kegs on sale are past day five. Do it every count. If the same two lines are always past day five, you've found where your fob and your drip-tray loss are coming from, before it turns up as a draught variance.

It ties straight into the numbers. Old kegs fob, fob gets poured away or topped up, and the stocktake shows fewer pints sold than the keg should have given. If your draught yield is short, check this before anything else, because it's the cheapest fix there is. Fobbing causes are in why is my beer fobbing, and what a normal draught loss looks like is in draught beer wastage: what's normal.

The basics that make five days achievable

  • Cellar at 11 to 13°C. Cask Marque's range, checked daily. Too warm and keg fobs whatever its age.
  • 48 hours in the cellar before connecting. That's how long Tennent's says a keg can take to reach temperature.
  • Lines cleaned every seven days. Yeast in the lines causes fob on its own, so you can't tell an old keg from a dirty line. My line cleaning guide has the routine.
  • Oldest on first. First in, first out, with 14 days of best-before at delivery.

Common questions

How long does a keg last once tapped?

In a pub cellar on gas, aim to sell it within about five days of putting it on. Cask Marque advises stocking keg brands so each keg sells within five days, and Tennent's says that after five days you will notice changes in taste and aroma, with more fobbing or flat beer.

How long does a cask last once tapped?

About three days on sale. Cask Marque's cellar card says to order container sizes you can empty within three days of placing them on sale, using mixed sizes if you need to.

Does keg beer go off once opened?

It goes downhill rather than going off overnight. The five-day guidance is about quality: the beer absorbs gas, the taste and aroma change and it fobs or pours flat. The date on the keg is a best-before for the sealed container, not a countdown once it is connected.

How many pints a day does a keg need to sell?

To empty an 88-pint keg in five days you need about 18 pints a day. A 30 litre keg, about 53 pints, needs about 11 a day, and a 20 litre keg, about 35 pints, needs 7. A 72-pint firkin of cask needs 24 a day to finish in three days.

How long can an unopened keg be stored?

Until the best-before date on it, kept in a cool cellar. Cask Marque's cellar card says not to accept a delivery with less than 14 days of best-before left, and to rotate stock so the oldest goes on first.
