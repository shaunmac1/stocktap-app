How many glasses of wine in a bottle? The 125, 175 and 250ml answer, and the 50ml most pubs lose

Shaun McManus 13 November 2026

A standard 75cl bottle of wine gives you six 125ml glasses, three 250ml glasses, or four 175ml glasses with 50ml left over. That 50ml is the bit worth knowing about. Across a 12-bottle case it's another three and a bit medium glasses, so a case is 51 glasses at 175ml, not 48.

Most of the pages that answer this are written for someone pouring at home, so they stop at "about five". Behind a bar you need the exact number, because it's what the stocktake expects you to have sold. Below is every bottle size, what each glass costs you, what to charge, the duty in it, and a two-minute check that tells you whether your wine is going where the till says it went.

Sparkling poured at 125ml works the same way: six flutes from a 75cl bottle.

Why it's 125, 175 and 250

Still wine sold by the glass in a pub has to be sold in 125ml or 175ml, or multiples of those, and the 125ml has to be on offer and made known to customers, on the menu, the wine list or a notice. A 250ml glass is two 125ml measures, which is why it's allowed. The glasses or the measure you pour with have to be the stamped, legal kind. Trading Standards' Business Companion sets this out plainly, and it's in the sources at the bottom.

So the 175ml everyone orders is the awkward one. It doesn't go into 750 evenly, and every bottle you open for mediums leaves you a short glass at the bottom.

The 50ml problem

Here's where wine quietly walks. You pour four 175s, there's a splash left, and it goes one of three ways. It starts the next glass from a fresh bottle, which is right. It gets tipped into the fourth glass as a kindness, which feels generous and costs you. Or it sits in the fridge door until it's past it and goes down the sink.

Take a house white that costs you £6 a bottle ex VAT and sells at £5.60 for a 175ml. The 50ml is two sevenths of a glass, so about £1.60 of sales a bottle, and 40p of cost. If two cases a week go through mediums and the last 50ml never gets sold, that's about seven glasses a week, £38 of sales, a bit under £2,000 a year. My arithmetic on an illustrative price, but swap in your own and it rarely comes out small.

The fix isn't telling anyone off. It's a house rule that the end of a bottle always starts the next glass, and a measure or lined glass so a 175 is a 175. If the same wine is open for days, the bigger loss is the bottle that dies, which is in my wine wastage guide, with the pump-or-argon question in wine preservation for pubs.

What each glass costs you, and what to charge

Work it from the bottle. A bottle that costs £6 ex VAT is £1.00 for every 125ml, £1.40 for every 175ml and £2.00 for every 250ml. To hit a 70% gross profit, divide the cost by 0.3 to get the ex-VAT price, then add 20% VAT.

Lots of lists price the large glass a bit cheaper per ml than the bottle to push upsell, and lots price the bottle cheaper to push bottles. Either is a choice. Just make it on purpose. The duty and units in each glass

Wine between 8.5% and 22% pays duty on the alcohol in it: £30.62 a litre of pure alcohol now, rising to £32.01 from 1 February 2027 after the Budget. So a stronger wine pays more duty, glass for glass. At 13%:

The units sum is the NHS one: ABV times millilitres, divided by 1,000. A 175ml of 13% is 13 x 175 ÷ 1,000 = 2.3 units. A bottle at 12% is 9 units, at 14.5% it's nearly 11. Worth knowing when a regular asks why the Rioja seems to hit harder than the Pinot Grigio. Units for beer and spirits are in how many units in a pint.

Counting open bottles at the stocktake

A 75cl bottle split into tenths is 75ml a tenth. So a 175ml is 2.3 tenths and a 125ml is 1.7. When you count an open bottle with four tenths left, that's 300ml, which is one 175 and most of another. Tenths are quick but a bit of a guess on a dark green bottle. Weighing is better: wine is close to water, about 0.99g a ml, so weigh it, take off the empty bottle weight, and you've got the millilitres. The method is the same one I use for spirits in how to weigh spirits for a stocktake.

The two-minute wine yield check

This is the check I'd do, and I haven't seen it written down anywhere for pubs. Pick your best-selling house wine. On Monday, take last week's till report and multiply the glasses sold by their size: say 30 mediums and 8 larges is 5,250ml plus 2,000ml, 7,250ml. Divide by 750 and that's how many bottles the till says you used: 9.7. Now count how many bottles you actually got through, from the empties or from opening and closing stock.

If you went through 11, that's 1.3 bottles nobody paid for, about six medium glasses. Could be the 50ml going in the sink, could be heavy pouring, could be a bottle that turned and got binned, could be the wine in the gravy. You won't know which from the number. But you'll know there is one, and that's the start. If the kitchen is borrowing wine, bar to kitchen transfers is the fix, and it's a five-second habit.

For functions and parties

Work it in millilitres, not "four a bottle". Sixty guests at two medium glasses each is 120 glasses, 21,000ml, which is 28 bottles. Count it as four glasses a bottle and you'd order 30 and have two left over, which is fine if you can sell them, and money sat on a shelf if you can't. For a toast in 125ml flutes, it's six a bottle, so ten bottles of fizz for 60.

Common questions

How many glasses of wine in a bottle?

A standard 75cl bottle gives six 125ml glasses, three 250ml glasses, or four 175ml glasses with 50ml left over. Across a 12-bottle case that 50ml adds up to 51 medium glasses, not 48.

How many 175ml glasses are in a bottle of wine?

Four, with 50ml left. 750ml divided by 175ml is 4.29, so every bottle has two sevenths of a glass left in it after the fourth pour. Two bottles give eight glasses and 100ml, and seven bottles give exactly 30 glasses.

How many 250ml glasses are in a bottle of wine?

Exactly three. A 75cl bottle is 750ml, so three large glasses empty it with nothing left over.

How many glasses of wine in a magnum?

A 1.5 litre magnum gives twelve 125ml glasses, six 250ml glasses, or eight 175ml glasses with 100ml left over.

What wine measures does a pub have to serve?

Still wine sold by the glass has to be sold in 125ml or 175ml, or multiples of them, and the 125ml has to be available and made known to customers. 250ml is two 125ml measures, which is why it is allowed.

How many units are in a glass of wine?

At 13% ABV a 125ml glass is 1.6 units, a 175ml glass is 2.3 units and a 250ml glass is 3.3 units. A whole 75cl bottle at 13% is 9.75 units. The NHS formula is ABV times millilitres divided by 1,000.
