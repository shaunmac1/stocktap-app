BevSpot Alternative for UK Pubs: What It Actually Costs vs StockTap

Shaun McManus Licensee, Washington, Tyne and Wear 31 July 2026

If you’ve landed here searching “BevSpot alternative,” I’ll save you some scrolling: BevSpot is a solid US bar/restaurant inventory tool, but it wasn’t built for a UK pub, and its pricing page is a proper head-scratcher. Let me walk you through what I found when I actually went and checked it — today, not off some old screenshot — and where a UK-built tool like StockTap fits instead.

What BevSpot actually charges (checked live, 31 July 2026)

Here’s what’s on bevspot.com/pricing right now:

Plan Price What you get Catch
Free £0 Full feature access, “white glove” setup Usage-capped — roughly 3 inventories, 5 orders, 5 recipes before you’re pushed to pay
Starter $948/year (~£750) Inventory on any device, one-click ordering, unlimited users Billed annually, no monthly option shown
Standard $1,428/year (~£1,130) Starter + COGS tracking, recipe costing, smart cart Still annual-only
Pro $249/year (~£197), tagged “Most Popular” Automated variance, menu profitability, multi-location reporting This is genuinely what the page says — a “Most Popular” tier priced lower than the plan beneath it. There’s also a separate custom-quote “Pro” tier for enterprise accounts. Two products called Pro isn’t a translation issue, it’s just a confusing pricing page

I’m not having a go for the sake of it — I checked this again today so I’m not repeating stale info, and it’s still like that. If you’re trying to budget for next year and the page itself can’t tell you clearly what “Pro” costs, that’s a red flag before you’ve even signed up.

All of this is in dollars. There’s no UK entity, no VAT handling, and — this is the bit that actually matters day to day — no allowance for how British licensees run stock. BevSpot’s whole build assumes US bar culture: cocktail-heavy menus, keg costing in US formats, POS integrations skewed to Toast and Square. If your pub’s core business is cask ale, wet-led trade, and a till system like a UK EPoS provider, you’re bending their tool to fit you, not the other way round.

What BevSpot is actually good at (fair’s fair)

I looked at what’s ranking for “BevSpot alternative” and what people say when comparing it to rivals like Backbar. Credit where it’s due: BevSpot’s recipe costing and menu profitability reporting are genuinely mature — it’s been doing this since the mid-2010s and it shows. Reviewers on Capterra rate it well for time saved on stocktakes, and it plugs cleanly into Toast POS if that’s your setup. If you’re running a US-style cocktail bar syncing with Toast, it’s not a bad shout.

Where it falls down for a UK pub: no GBP pricing, US-centric recipe/keg units, contract-style annual billing with no visible monthly rolling option, and support hours built around US time zones — so if your delivery’s short at 6am on a Tuesday, you’re not getting a same-morning reply from Boston.

A worked example

Say you’re a single-site pub doing three deliveries a week, tracking maybe 80 lines across cask, keg, spirits and wines. On BevSpot, to get variance reporting and multi-location-style features you’re into “Pro” territory — which, per their own page today, is either £197/year or a custom quote, depending which “Pro” you land on. On StockTap’s founding rate, you’re looking at £19/month — £228/year — for a tool that speaks your stock language from day one, with no annual contract forcing your hand.

Where BevSpot still wins, honestly

If you’re multi-site in the US, already on Toast, and want deep enterprise reporting with a dedicated account manager, BevSpot’s enterprise tier can do things a young UK tool can’t yet — built-in accounting integrations at scale, for one. That’s a real gap. For a single UK pub or small local group, though, that complexity is a cost, not a feature.

Common questions

Is BevSpot available in the UK?

Yes, technically — nothing stops you signing up from a UK IP. But pricing is in USD, there’s no VAT-inclusive display, and the product’s built around US recipe/keg conventions rather than UK cask and keg stock-taking.

Does BevSpot have a free plan?

Yes, but it’s capped at roughly 3 inventories, 5 orders and 5 recipes (per bevspot.com/pricing, checked 31 July 2026) — fine for testing, not for running a real pub week to week.

Is StockTap’s £19/month rate a permanent price?

No — it’s the founding rate for the first 50 customers only, as a straightforward early-access offer, not a countdown gimmick. After that it moves to standard pricing.

What’s the biggest practical difference day to day?

Currency and stock units. BevSpot makes you translate your pub’s stock into a US-restaurant format. StockTap doesn’t, because it was built around a UK pub’s stock to begin with.
