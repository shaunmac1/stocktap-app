MarketMan alternative for UK pubs: right tool, wrong size

Shaun McManus 12 September 2026

MarketMan is proper software with a proper reputation in food-led operations, and it prices itself accordingly: $249 to $449 and up a month, in dollars, agreed on a demo call. If you run a UK pub and someone's told you MarketMan is the answer to your stock problems, this is what it actually costs in pounds, what it does well, what it doesn't do at all, and where a pub gets the same job done for a fraction of the money.

I run a wet-led pub in Washington, Tyne and Wear, and I've spent this year working through every stock system a pub might be sold. Some of them are bad software. MarketMan isn't. It's good software aimed at a different building.

What MarketMan actually is

MarketMan calls itself restaurant inventory management, and that's accurate. The centre of it is purchasing and food cost: supplier catalogues and ordering in one place, invoices scanned and pushed into your accounts, recipe costing down to the gram, cost-of-goods reporting across sites, and count sheets for the storeroom and walk-in. If your operation lives and dies on plate cost across four kitchens, that's a real toolset.

Notice what's not in that sentence: nothing about a cellar. Nothing about weighing an open bottle of Ketel One, calibrating an 11-gallon keg, dipping a cask, or explaining why a naive system counts legal froth as theft. It's not that MarketMan does these things badly. They're not the job it was built for.

The price, in the wrong currency

MarketMan's own pricing page, checked today, lists three plans: Starter at $249 a month, Growth at $299, Enterprise from $449. There's a free-setup offer they value at $1,500, add-on commissary modules at $499 to $749 a month, and API access billed separately by location count. No free trial. You book a demo and buy from a salesperson.

Two things about that for a UK buyer. First, the obvious one: those are dollars. At any exchange rate you've seen lately, $249 lands somewhere around £185 to £200 a month before your card provider adds its currency fee. Second, the price you actually pay is settled on a call, and contract terms aren't published anywhere on the pricing page. I can't tell you what the tie-in is, and that's rather the point. Anyone confident in their product puts the leaving door in writing.

For scale: I've written up what pub stocktaking software costs in 2026, and the monthly apps a single site should be looking at run from around a tenner to the fifties. MarketMan starts at three to four times the top of that range.

What it does well, credit where due

The invoice side is the real strength. Supplier invoices get scanned, line prices get tracked, and when your butter goes up 9% you find out from a report instead of noticing three months later that your food GP quietly slid. Recipe costing is the other one: if you need to know that the fish pie costs £3.41 to plate this week against £3.18 last month, MarketMan will tell you, and StockTap won't, because recipe costing isn't something we do.

Multi-site purchasing is the third leg. Central kitchens, franchise groups, an ops manager approving orders across sites. That's who the commissary modules and the API pricing are for, and at that scale $299 a month is a rounding error against what a percentage point of food cost is worth.

What a pub needs that isn't in there

A pub's stock problem is mostly a wet problem. Draught that legally pours with a head, kegs you can't see inside, casks that lose sediment and tasting pulls, spirits that drift a nip at a time across forty optics. The tools for that job are specific: bottle weighing so open spirits are measured instead of guessed, keg and cask calibration so a dip reading means something, and variance that understands what's normal in a UK pub rather than an American restaurant bar.

MarketMan counts by count sheet, units and cases. That's fine for the storeroom and it's fine for a kitchen. Point it at a back bar and you're eyeballing tenths into an expensive American purchasing system, which is the same guess you'd make on a free spreadsheet, dressed up.

If it's the food side you care about, I've written a separate honest piece on restaurant stock control for UK operators, because the answer there is different.

The pints arithmetic

Here's the sum I run on every tool in this series. The average UK pint made about £2.23 of gross profit in 2026 on my arithmetic from the Morning Advertiser's £5.34 average price. Call MarketMan Starter £190 a month at current rates. That's £43.85 a week, which is 20 pints of GP a week, every week, before the software has paid for itself. Growth is closer to 24. StockTap Premium at £39 a month needs 4.

That's not a trick sum. A four-site food group really can save 20 pints of GP a week through tighter purchasing alone. A single wet-led pub almost certainly can't, because a single wet-led pub's leak isn't in supplier catalogues. It's in the cellar and behind the optics, which is the half MarketMan doesn't count.

So who should actually use MarketMan?

Food-led groups with three or more kitchens, and operators whose margin lives on plate cost rather than pour cost. If that's you, book their demo with a clear conscience, and ask them to put the contract length and the total first-year cost in writing before you sign anything.

If you're a UK pub, tied or free, whose money is mostly in draught and spirits, you're the wrong customer at the wrong price in the wrong currency. Compare MarginEdge and Jelly if you're shopping this end of the market, or the full side-by-side comparison if you want the whole field on one page.

Common questions

How much does MarketMan cost in the UK?

The published plans are $249, $299 and from $449 a month, in US dollars, which is roughly £185 to £355 at recent exchange rates before card currency fees. The final price is agreed on a demo call and contract terms aren't published.

Does MarketMan have a free trial?

No. It's demo-led sales with a free-setup offer rather than a self-serve trial. If you want to test a system on your own stock this week without talking to anyone, that alone rules it out.

Does MarketMan handle draught beer and spirits properly?

It counts them the way it counts everything, by count sheets and units. There's nothing built around bottle weighing, keg or cask calibration, or UK draught allowances. For a wet-led site that's the whole job, so it matters.

What's the best MarketMan alternative for a UK pub?

Depends which half of MarketMan you wanted. For recipe costing and invoice automation in a food-led site, look at Jelly or MarginEdge and check the prices carefully. For the wet side, counting, weighing and variance in pounds, that's exactly what StockTap does, £39 a month with a free trial and no card.
