Digital ID at the bar from 15 September 2026: what you can accept, and what you still can't

Shaun McManus 16 September 2026

Since Tuesday, a phone can legally be proof of age at the bar in England and Wales. Nobody has to accept one, physical ID still works, and Challenge 25 hasn't moved an inch. But if your staff are already waving through anyone who flashes an ID app at them, that's the version that ends with a review of your licence.

What changed on 15 September

The Licensing Act 2003 (Mandatory Licensing Conditions) (Amendment) Order 2026 came into force on 15 September 2026. From that date, licensed premises can accept digital proof of age when selling alcohol, provided it comes from a certified digital verification service, a DVS, listed on the government's statutory register.

Accepting one is not a matter of looking at a screen. The check has to happen through technology, what the government calls a programmatic check: the customer's phone shows a QR code or taps against a reader, and your device confirms the result. Your member of staff gets a clear pass or fail on their own kit, not the customer's. The customer doesn't have to share a name or address either, just the fact they're over 18, which is all you ever needed to know anyway.

What hasn't changed

Everything else. The drinking age is still 18. Challenge 25 still decides when you ask. A passport or driving licence stays valid, and the government has been clear it will remain so. Your refusals record still needs the date, the time and the name of the staff member who said no, same as it did last week, and if that record is a mystery in your building, start with the incident book guide before you worry about any of this.

And none of it is compulsory. This is a permission, not an obligation. A pub that never accepts a digital ID is breaking no rule at all. The digital government minister put it plainly: whether to adopt it stays the business's choice, and physical ID remains a valid way for customers to prove their age.

The mistake that will cost somebody a licence

Here's the bit to brief the team on before the weekend. Glancing at an ID app on someone's phone is not a digital age check. The government's own guidance says licence holders must not rely on visual checks of a digital proof of age, because convincing fakes of an app screen are easy to make. A screenshot of someone else's pass screen takes a teenager about thirty seconds.

So the rule at the bar is simple. Either you have kit that scans or taps and puts the confirmation on your device, or the answer stays what it has always been: physical ID please. There is no middle position, and no due diligence defence built out of squinting at somebody's screen.

Should you bother yet?

Depends what you run. For a big venue with a door team and a Saturday queue, faster checks are worth real money, and 72% of respondents to the government's 2024 consultation backed digital ID for alcohol purchases, so a fair chunk of your younger customers will turn up expecting it. Plenty of them stopped carrying a wallet years ago.

For a wet-led local where the same forty faces drink every week, I'd wait. The order only came into force this week. Let your EPOS supplier or scanner firm tell you what they support and what it costs, and let venues with more to gain find the teething problems first. UKHospitality welcomed the change but pointedly asked government to keep the rollout cheap and unburdensome for operators, which tells you the cost question isn't settled yet.

If you do adopt it, two jobs before the first check. Confirm the service you're relying on is actually listed on the DVS register on GOV.UK, because only registered services count under the order. Then train one sentence into every member of staff: if the phone can't be scanned, it's physical ID, no exceptions. Write the adoption date and the provider into your due diligence file while you're at it. The paper trail is the point.

Last thing worth knowing: this is England and Wales only. The Licensing Act 2003 doesn't cover Scotland or Northern Ireland, so nothing changes there for now.
