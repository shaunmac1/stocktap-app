Money & Numbers

How many pubs are there in the UK in 2026? The real numbers

Shaun McManus 22 August 2026

There are roughly 44,500 pubs in the UK in mid 2026. The British Beer and Pub Association counted 44,656 at the end of 2025, and pubs have been closing at nearly two a day since. Anyone who gives you one exact number is picking a source and not telling you they picked it. Here are all the numbers, where each one comes from, and why they don't agree.

I run a pub in Washington, Tyne and Wear, so this isn't an academic question for me. Every one of these closures is a building like mine with a cellar like mine and a set of margins that stopped working. I keep this page updated as new figures land, and every figure on it has a named source underneath.

The three counts, and why they disagree

Nobody holds a single register of British pubs. Three different organisations count, they count different things, and their answers are thousands apart.

The gap between the BBPA and Lumina isn't an error. The BBPA counts pubs as the trade understands them. Lumina runs an outlet census with its own definition of what qualifies as a pub or bar, and it counted 41,171 outlets in its 2026 report, down 0.9% on the year. The Commons Library briefing leans on ONS business counts, which track VAT and PAYE registered businesses rather than buildings with a licence, so a company running six sites can show up once. Same country, three honest answers.

If you just want a number for a quiz: say around 44,500 and cite the BBPA. It's the count the industry itself uses.

How fast are pubs closing in 2026?

Faster than last year. BBPA figures show 161 pubs closed in the first quarter of 2026, which is nearly two a day and a 26% jump on the 128 that shut in the same quarter of 2025. Around 2,400 jobs went with them, roughly half of them younger workers.

Zoom out and the shape is steady erosion rather than a cliff. The BBPA's five year run shows a net loss of close to 2,200 pubs, from 46,829 down to 44,656. Net closures across 2025 alone came to 336. Lumina's census works out at about seven net closures a week on its own definition. Go back further and the Commons Library puts the fall from 2000 to 2019 at 13,600 pubs, a 22% drop, before the pandemic took its share.

Why the closures are speeding up

The BBPA's own position is that trade is brisk and the tax burden is what's wiping profits out, and from behind a bar I'd say that matches what I see. The cost side of running a pub moved sharply in the year to April 2026. The National Living Wage went to £12.71 an hour, up 4.1%. Employer National Insurance sits at 15% with the threshold down at £5,000. Alcohol duty went up 3.66% on 1 February 2026. Every one of those lands on a wet-led pub before it sells a single extra pint.

There is one piece of genuine relief coming: business rates relief for eligible pubs and clubs in England rises from 15% to 35% from April 2027, worth around £1,100 a year to a typical venue, across roughly 32,000 sites. Welcome, but it's a fraction of what the wage and duty rises added.

What the headline number hides

A pub doesn't close on the day the doors shut. It closes over the eighteen months before that, quietly, while the licensee doesn't have a clear picture of the margins. Busy and profitable are not the same thing. I've had quarters where the tills sang and the stock result still came back hundreds of pounds down, and my own liquor audits between December 2025 and July 2026 swung from £193 up to £756 down with no obvious change in trade.

That's the part of the closure statistics nobody prints. The tax burden is real and it's not in any licensee's control. The wet margin is real too, and it is. A pub that knows its GP% every week has a fighting chance of fixing a leak while it's still a leak. A pub that finds out at the quarterly stocktake is reading history. I wrote up what the closure numbers teach about margins in the margin lessons from 2026's pub closures, and the full market picture is in my breakdown of the Lumina 2026 pub market report.

Quick answers

How many pubs are there in the UK in 2026?

About 44,500 on the BBPA's count: 44,656 at the end of 2025, minus a first quarter of 2026 that closed 161 more. Lumina's stricter outlet census says 41,171.

How many pubs are closing per day?

Nearly two a day in the first quarter of 2026, on BBPA figures. Net of new openings across a full year the loss is slower: 336 net closures in 2025.

How many pubs has the UK lost?

13,600 between 2000 and 2019 on the Commons Library's figures, and close to another 2,200 net in the last five years on the BBPA's.

Is the market shrinking in money terms too?

No, and that's the strange part. Lumina puts the pub and bar market up 2% to £24.7bn in 2026 while outlet numbers fall. Fewer sites, taking more each. The pubs that survive are bigger businesses than the ones that closed.
